## Speaking Script

### 1. Introduction & The Problem
> "Good morning everyone. Today I’m presenting **BEYOND LABELS**, a web platform designed around one core idea: *abilities and personalities have no gender.*
>
> Every day, without even realizing it, we absorb rules about how men and women are supposed to look, act, and live. Decades ago, vintage household ads taught people that cleaning and caregiving belonged only to women, while leadership belonged to men. Today, social media algorithms do the same thing—pushing beauty filters, rigid fitness ideals, and double standards into our feeds.
>
> **BEYOND LABELS** uses the same visual power of media—clear storytelling, photography, and shareable design—to challenge those stereotypes."

### 2. Overview of Features
> "Our website brings together **8 interactive features** across **6 sections**, turning passive readers into active participants. Let me walk you through how each feature works and how it helps society."

### 3. Awareness & Media Literacy (Features 1–3)
> "**First, the Hero Live Flip Card.** Right at the top of the page, visitors can click one button to flip a flashcard from an *Old Expectation*—styled like a 1950s ad—to *Modern Reality*, showing partners sharing home life equally. It immediately hooks the user with visual contrast.
>
> **Second, the 'Why It Matters' 3-Pillar Breakdown.** Using real editorial photography, it explains the root causes: traditional media scripts, social media echo chambers, and their real-world impact—such as men feeling pressured to hide emotions or online creators being judged by gender instead of skill.
>
> **Third, the 'Catch the Stereotype' Evidence-Backed Reality Check Quiz.** Users read 4 real-world social media scenarios and decide whether each one reinforces an outdated stereotype or models positive representation. To prevent personal or biased opinions, every single quiz answer is backed by **peer-reviewed academic research papers with direct DOI links**—such as Eisend (2010) on advertising gender roles, Wong et al. (2017) on masculinity norms and mental health, Fox & Tang (2017) on online gaming harassment, and Dasgupta & Asgari (2004) on counter-stereotypical role models. This builds **evidence-based media literacy**."

### 4. Visual Advocacy & Shareable Tools (Features 4–6)
> "**Fourth, the Interactive Infographic Studio.** It features **5 core flashcards** covering *Home & Caregiving*, *Leadership & Careers*, *Strength & Emotion*, *Body & Beauty*, and *Creators & Culture*. Users can filter by category or switch between Side-by-Side comparison and Modern View.
>
> **Fifth, the Customize & Export Modal.** Awareness shouldn’t stay trapped on one website. Users can customize any card in **3 pastel palettes** (*Sage Meadow*, *Warm Blush*, *Soft Sky*), preview it in **4:5 Instagram Feed** or **9:16 Story** format, copy a ready-to-post caption, and download a high-resolution PNG to share on their own social media.
>
> **Sixth, the Personal Pledge Badge Generator.** At the bottom of the page, users pick a personal commitment—like *'I judge skills, character, and effort—never gender'*—and download a square **Pledge Badge PNG** to post online."

### 5. Reddit-Style Community, Photos & Login System (Features 7–8)
> "**Seventh, our Reddit-style Community Forum (`r/BeyondLabels`) and Photo Stories Wall.** Just like Reddit, members can create discussion posts with topic flairs, attach supporting sources, upvote or downvote perspectives, and reply in nested comment threads—alongside uploading real-life photo stories.
>
> **Eighth, the Member Login & Profile System.** Users can create an account with a custom profile photo (or try the 1-click Demo Account). Once logged in, they can **bookmark** their favorite infographic cards, **save their Reality Check quiz progress**, **vote and comment in the forum**, and **upload their own photo stories**."

### 6. How This Helps Society & Conclusion
> "So, how does **BEYOND LABELS** help solve gender stereotype problems in society? In three practical ways:
> 1. **It builds critical thinking:** The Reality Check quiz trains people—especially young social media users—to recognize harmful tropes instead of accepting them as normal.
> 2. **It replaces narrow scripts with positive representation:** Real photography and modern comparisons show that caregiving, leadership, vulnerability, and creativity belong to everyone.
> 3. **It turns empathy into shareable action:** By giving people ready-to-download infographics, pledge badges, and a community photo wall, it empowers everyday users to spread healthier messages in their own feeds.
>
> When we stop boxing people into gender labels, everyone gets more room to be authentic, respected, and free to grow. Thank you, and I’d be happy to show a quick live demo or answer any questions."
